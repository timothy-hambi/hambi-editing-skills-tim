# Hambi Editing Standards

These rules apply to every HP edit unless the ticket or the client's Brand Hub says otherwise.

**Order of authority:**

1. The ticket.
2. The client's Brand Hub.
3. This file.

Where they conflict, follow the higher source and note the conflict in the handoff.

The rules are ordered by how often reviewers flagged each issue. Sections 1–3 cover about two-thirds of all internal feedback.

---

## 1. Captions and on-screen text

### 1a. Accuracy (check every caption before export)

- **C1.** Captions match the spoken audio word for word. Fix missing, doubled and swapped words.
- **C2.** Spelling, grammar and punctuation are correct. The usual misses are:
  - hyphens in compound words (half-used, five-star, 3-second, money-back)
  - commas in lists ("YOUR SKIN, YOUR HAIR AND YOUR NAILS")
  - possessives and their/they're/there (yours, their)
  - stray, unpaired or misplaced quote marks
  - spaces before punctuation, and double spaces
- **C3.** Captions and overlays spell a word the same way. If the overlay says HYDROLYSED, the caption can't say HYDROLISED.
- **C4.** Product and brand names are exact: spelling, spacing and case (Bella+Duke, Pore Favor, Complete Gut by Biomel). Brand names always appear in captions.
- **C5.** Use numerals in captions (3, not three). The exception is a single 0 or 1 standing alone, where it could be misread as O, l or I.
- **C6.** UK clients use UK spelling (Oestrogen, colour, moisturise, sulphates) and £, never $.
- **C7.** Captions follow the approved script's punctuation and wording. Never paraphrase approved copy.
- **C8.** Captions use correct sentence grammar, even though each cue is a single line. Keep full stops, commas, question marks and capitals; never strip them off the end of a cue. When a sentence spans several cues, its punctuation sits on the cue where it falls, and the cue that starts the next sentence opens with a capital (e.g. "with acne." then "It's the photos"). Where the punctuation comes from depends on the line:
  - **Line said word for word from the ticket script:** copy the script's punctuation and capitals exactly (C7).
  - **Line changed on the shoot** (words added, dropped or reworded): apply standard English grammar to what was actually said.

### 1b. Styling and placement

- **S1.** Use the brand fonts from the Brand Hub for both captions and overlays. Never mix fonts that aren't in the brand set.
- **S2.** Text must be readable on a phone over any background. Use a box, stroke or soft, diffuse shadow. Never use harsh hard-edged shadows, and avoid low-contrast text.
- **S3.** Size text to fit. Big enough to read, small enough not to dominate the shot. Graphics and text boxes are 2 lines maximum, wrapped tightly with no wasted empty space.
- **S4.** All text sits inside platform safe zones (9:16 Meta/TikTok UI) and is centred horizontally. On split screens, captions sit on the centre seam.
- **S5.** Text never covers the product, the pack label, people's faces, the CTA badge or key on-screen detail. Move it up or down rather than letting it collide.
- **S6.** Show one message at a time. Hide captions while block copy, a banner or a headline is on screen. Drop overlays that just repeat the VO.
- **S7.** Use sentence case unless the brand specifies caps. Use native iPhone/Apple-style emojis only, never clip-art.
- **S8.** Only animate text with the style the brand has approved. Don't add pop-ins, bounces or transitions the brand doesn't use.
- **S9.** Hold text on screen long enough to read: about 2 s minimum for stats, graphs and ingredient lists.
- **S10.** Keep persistent banners (offers, Black Friday) at the top of frame for the whole video, never over the captions. Remove them on the end card only.

## 2. B-roll and visuals

- **B1.** **On-story or out.** Every B-roll shot illustrates the exact line being spoken at that moment. If a shot doesn't connect to the words, cut back to the presenter.
- **B2.** **Source priority:**
  1. The client's HP library and previous shoots.
  2. Approved UGC.
  3. UGC/iPhone-style stock (Envato, Pexels).
  4. AI.
  Stock must not look like a glossy commercial.
- **B3.** **AI must not look AI.** Reject clips with warped faces, over-smooth skin, unnatural movement or an obviously fake background. Use a still image or real footage instead.
- **B4.** Use fewer, longer, varied shots rather than a new clip every few words. Never repeat the same B-roll in one ad unless it's a deliberate callback.
- **B5.** Break up talking-head stretches longer than about 5 s with B-roll, split screen (talking head top, B-roll bottom) or B-cam. Don't fake variety with repeated digital zooms.
- **B6.** **Current packaging and the correct variant only.** Never show old packaging or the wrong product (e.g. Lumos when the line is about Wren). Put the product on screen on the word that names it.
- **B7.** Show the product clearly and prominently. It shouldn't be cropped, covered, blurry or held awkwardly.
- **B8.** Use clean cuts by default. Only use zooms, punch-ins, transitions and effects (flame or colour washes) when the ticket asks for them. A zoom is fine to reframe on the subject or to remove empty space.
- **B9.** Graphics must look native to the brand: brand colours and fonts, rounded boxes, and no default PowerPoint-style charts. Keep them sharp, never pixelated.
- **B10.** Match on-screen visuals to the wording. A "meal deal" must look like a UK meal deal, a "scoop" must be a scoop, and the "fridge" line needs a fridge.
- **B11.** Pick talent and B-roll subjects that match the target persona's age and look (e.g. women aged 40–50 for a 46-year-old pain point).
- **B12.** **Multi-camera shoots:** when the shot is on one person, use the B-cam (or whichever camera is framed on that person). Use the A-cam full frame (wide) only in split screens. Only crop the A-cam to one person when no other camera covers that moment, and flag it. (Added 2026-11-25)

## 3. Cuts and pacing

- **P1.** Remove dead air, filler words, false starts and forced reactions ("wow yeah", "Awww"). Use J-cuts across gaps so the next line comes in early.
- **P2.** **Never clip a word.** Check every cut point at full volume. Leave a natural breath after key lines so the delivery doesn't feel rushed.
- **P3.** Cut filler lines that only repeat the previous point.
- **P4.** Start each hook on the clean take where the line lands, not on a repeat.
- **P5.** Follow the script line order. Before export, check the cut against the ticket line by line for missing or re-ordered lines.
- **P6.** On-screen text, product shots and B-roll land on the word that mentions them, not before.
- **P7.** If the runtime is long, bring the product shot and CTA in while the presenter is still speaking.
- **P8.** End cleanly: no black frames, no empty tail, and no captions running past the final line.
- **P9.** Cut every off-camera voice: slate countdowns ("three, two, one"), crew or director talk, and off-screen comments. Check the head and tail of every take, because a countdown's last word often sits right before the first line. (Added 2026-11-25)

## 4. Hooks

- **H1.** The hook must work with the sound off. Add a hook text overlay at the top that states the pain point or curiosity.
- **H2.** Open on the strongest visual. For an expert or founder, show them large and chest-up for the first 3–5 s before shrinking or cutting to B-roll. For product-led hooks, put the payoff first.
- **H3.** Give static hooks motion or variety: quick cuts of several before/afters, or a movement beat.

## 5. Colour and image quality

- **G1.** Grade every shot to one look. Match B-roll to the A-roll's grade, and default to warm and premium. Fix grey, desaturated, too-dark or too-bright shots.
- **G2.** Only adjust saturation and brightness where it's needed. Don't apply a global boost that over-brightens shots that were already fine.
- **G3.** Fix technical faults before export: glitches, misaligned layers, tilted framing, low-quality or pixelated clips, and a dirty-lens look (lift the contrast).

## 6. Audio

- **A1.** Music fits the brand and the brief's mood. Prefer tracks already used in client-approved concepts. Avoid sad, cheesy or orchestral "inspirational" tracks unless briefed.
- **A2.** The VO always sits clearly above the music: duck music under speech, roughly −20 to −25% under VO. Even out levels across speakers and interviews.
- **A3.** Normalise the final mix. If an export sounds quiet, lift it (target about −14 LUFS integrated for social).
- **A4.** Clean the dialogue. Remove reverb and echo with Adobe Podcast or Auphonic, and check the hook for stray sounds.
- **A5.** AI VO matches the client's locale: a UK accent for UK brands, UK pronunciation (vitamin, oestrogen), and brand names said the client's way.
- **A6.** Only use SFX on purpose: to land a beat (stamp, ding, whoosh, money, typing). Remove random SFX, and follow brand rules (some brands want none).
- **A7.** Foley must sync with the picture, e.g. pen-on-paper sound matches the pen.

## 7. CTA, offer and end card

- **E1.** Use the current CTA and end card from the Brand Hub or ticket, in the right version for the product or offer.
- **E2.** Offer text, prices and roundels match the ticket exactly (e.g. "15% off", "£1.76 a day").
- **E3.** The end card includes a clear shop-now action, plus trust signals (reviews, rating badge) where the brand uses them.
- **E4.** Remove captions from the CTA or end card unless the brand wants them.

## 8. Compliance and brand safety

- **K1.** Blur third-party brand names and labels, and personal names in screenshots.
- **K2.** Respect category limits. For example: no alcohol or hangover references for supplements; no doctor cues (lab coat, "Dr") on AI experts unless approved; and ASA-safe claims.
- **K3.** Add a substantiation line ("Source: …") wherever a stat or survey figure is shown.
- **K4.** Remove sensitive content from screen recordings (search suggestions, notifications).

## Pre-export checklist

Run this before every export. Any skill producing an edit must report pass or fail on each item.

1. [ ] Every caption has been checked against the audio, with sentence grammar kept: the script's punctuation for verbatim lines, standard grammar for lines changed on the shoot (C1–C8).
2. [ ] Brand fonts are used, and all text is inside safe zones and centred (S1, S4).
3. [ ] No text covers the product, faces or the CTA (S5).
4. [ ] Every B-roll shot is on-story; there's no old packaging, wrong variant or obvious AI (B1, B3, B6).
5. [ ] No clipped words, dead air or missing script lines (P2, P5).
6. [ ] The hook has a sound-off text overlay and opens on the strongest visual (H1, H2).
7. [ ] There's one consistent grade and no glitches (G1, G3).
8. [ ] VO sits above the music, levels are even and the mix is normalised (A2, A3).
9. [ ] The CTA, offer and end card match the ticket and Brand Hub (E1, E2).
10. [ ] Compliance blurs and substantiation lines are in place (K1–K3).

## Feedback loop (how this file grows)

- When review feedback applies across clients (e.g. "don't cut mid-sentence"), add it here as a new numbered rule in the right section, with the date.
- When feedback applies only to one client (fonts, colours, offers, pronunciation, banned visuals), it goes in that client's Brand Hub and the Client overrides below. Don't add it to sections 1–8.
- When feedback applies only to that one ad (swap this clip, change this word), log it on the ticket. It never goes here.
- When unsure whether a rule is general, propose it to Tim for approval before adding it.
